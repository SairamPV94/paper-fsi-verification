The following claims need qualification or additional evidence:

The methodological issues are:

17. **Agreement between coupling algorithms is not an upper bound on either algorithm’s iterative error.** [sectionCrossBenchmark.tex:164](sectionCrossBenchmark.tex:164) says agreement “bounds the iterative error,” repeated in [sectionVerificationMethodology.tex:115](sectionVerificationMethodology.tex:115). Both algorithms can share an incomplete field solve or stopping error; conversely, the manuscript acknowledges different converged discrete interface conditions. **Fix:** describe agreement as an internal consistency check, and establish iterative-error estimates through within-algorithm tolerance/corrector sweeps at fixed discretization.

18. **Coarse-level sensitivity checks are promoted into fine-level error bounds without demonstrated transferability.** [sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:450](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:450) explicitly bounds 4x iterative error using a 2x pair; the corrected 8x′ comparison uses time and tolerance checks from 2x′ and 1x′ ([line 386](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:386)). The paper itself shows tolerance sensitivity increasing with refinement ([line 452](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:452)). Similarly, Mok’s negligible-error conclusion covers successful 32²/64² repeats although the 96² strict run fails ([line 598](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:598)). **Fix:** restrict each conclusion to tested resolutions or obtain fine-level checks; describe transferred estimates as assumptions rather than bounds.

19. **FSI1 uses Richardson/GCI inconsistently with the stated policy and compares the wrong centres.** The methodology requires behaviour compatible with the expected asymptotic order and names only Hessenthaler studies as GCI applications ([sectionVerificationMethodology.tex:81](sectionVerificationMethodology.tex:81), [line 97](sectionVerificationMethodology.tex:97)); FSI1 nevertheless extrapolates orders near 1 and 1.5 and invokes GCI ([sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:425](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:425)). More seriously, it compares the **extrapolated** \(u_x\)’s 0.52% discrepancy with the **finest-grid** GCI of 0.42%. **Fix:** state an explicit policy for stable sub-nominal sequences, label these extrapolations accordingly, and centre the finest-grid GCI on the finest-grid value—not the Richardson value.

20. **Successive changes are repeatedly treated as remaining-error bounds.** For example, [sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:528](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:528) infers level-3 uncertainty “at least approximately 0.6%” from the last 3dTube increment despite growing differences; [line 324](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:324) assigns FSI3 reference uncertainties even though [line 326](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:326) correctly says no error bound can be formed. A mesh increment alone is neither a lower nor an upper bound on the finest solution’s error. **Fix:** report these as observed sensitivity scales and state that remaining discretization error is unquantified.

21. **The Hessenthaler solid order includes a mesh-triggered defect, and its correction is transferred between fluid meshes without direct verification.** The reported order 2.21/GCI 0.034 mm uses the uncorrected S3 value ([sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:670](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:670)); the evidence says the interpolation defect affects S3 but not S1/S2, and the +0.006-mm correction was measured only on F1S3 over a 750-step restart ([hessenthaler_phaseI_verification.md:393](paper-data/hessenthaler_phaseI_verification.md:393)). **Fix:** verify the correction’s applicability to F3S3 and recompute the solid convergence measures with one consistent operator; applying the stated correction already changes the estimated order to approximately 2.13 and GCI to 0.039 mm.

Likewise define the arrival event and probe-based estimator behind \(t_{\mathrm{arr}}\) and \(c_p\) at [line 513](sectionBenchmarkSuite.tex:513).
